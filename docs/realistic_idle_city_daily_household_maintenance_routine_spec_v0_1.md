# Purpose

This document defines the early-game layer for:

- daily camp routine
- household maintenance
- recurring domestic labor
- readiness work that prevents larger failures
- transition from fragile camp to dependable camp life

The existing design stack already covers:
- tasks
- priorities
- logistics
- sanitation
- water
- fire
- sleep
- food handling
- health
- storage
- weather pressure

What is still missing is one focused document that answers:

- what people actually do every day to keep a camp livable
- why “maintenance” is not side flavor but core survival labor
- how repeated domestic work becomes player-visible progress
- how routine separates a surviving camp from a failing one

---

# 1. High-level stance

## 1.1 Domestic work is part of the game’s main loop

The game should not treat household work as invisible housekeeping.

In the early game, routine labor is survival labor.

That includes:
- water fetching and staging
- fire tending
- drying and rotating bedding
- cleaning / tidying
- waste handling
- protecting food and fuel
- checking storage condition
- preparing for night
- preparing for weather

## 1.2 A camp becomes “real” when it has routines

A camp should not feel mature just because it has more objects.

A camp feels real when:
- people know where things belong
- essentials are checked before failure
- evening and morning burdens are manageable
- storms do not instantly expose total disorder
- one bad event does not destroy all function

This document defines the routines that create that feeling.

## 1.3 Household work should compete with expansion

A key early-game truth is:
every hour spent on expansion is an hour not spent keeping the camp functional.

That tension should be playable.

# 2. Core design goals

The routine system should make the player care about:
- readiness
- order
- protection of essentials
- recurring labor burden
- quality of living space, not only production output
- who is quietly carrying the camp through thankless work
- when neglect is building up beneath visible progress

# 3. Main routine categories

## 3.1 Fire routine
Daily work includes:
- check ember / live-fire continuity
- stage kindling and dry fuel
- protect fuel from damp
- reposition or secure fire area if conditions demand

## 3.2 Water routine
Daily work includes:
- fetch water
- stage near-use containers
- separate raw vs safer water practice where relevant
- maintain enough evening / night water
- keep water handling zone orderly

## 3.3 Sleep routine
Daily work includes:
- inspect sleeping area
- air or dry bedding
- move damp material
- prepare a usable sleep setup before exhaustion peaks

## 3.4 Food routine
Daily work includes:
- inspect perishable items
- move food under cover
- separate safe from questionable food
- prepare near-term consumption
- prevent waste from exposure, animals, damp, or neglect

## 3.5 Sanitation routine
Daily work includes:
- maintain waste separation
- keep living / food areas cleaner than disposal zones
- wash hands / vessels where relevant
- manage foul buildup before it becomes a health threat

## 3.6 Storage routine
Daily work includes:
- put things back into the right area
- protect key materials from rain
- keep essentials findable
- stage tomorrow’s most-needed goods

## 3.7 Path / camp order routine
Daily work includes:
- clear obstructions
- reduce clutter around key flow areas
- keep critical routes usable in bad weather
- remove or reposition wasteful stockpiles

## 3.8 Body maintenance routine
Daily work includes:
- drying wraps and bedding
- checking footwear condition
- cleaning or isolating contaminated items
- ensuring vulnerable people are not sleeping in the worst conditions

# 4. Day structure for routine play

This is not a rigid schedule for every NPC.
It is the intended rhythm of camp life.

## 4.1 Dawn / first activity window
Common tasks:
- check fire
- fetch or assess water
- assess weather
- restore order after the night
- determine whether emergency maintenance overrides daytime plans

## 4.2 Main work window
Common tasks:
- gathering
- hauling
- construction
- processing
- scouting
- repairs

Routine pressure still matters here because:
- neglected basics create evening collapse
- weather can force an early return to maintenance
- some domestic tasks are best done in daylight

## 4.3 Late-day / return window
Common tasks:
- bring essentials back under cover
- stage fuel
- secure water
- sort gathered goods
- air or dry items if possible
- make sure sleep is not going to fail

This should be one of the most important windows in the early game.

## 4.4 Night / reduced activity window
Common tasks:
- maintain safe minimal fire
- rest
- emergency care
- limited reaction to sudden failures

A camp with poor late-day routine should suffer at night.

# 5. Routine as a burden system

## 5.1 Every camp has a daily maintenance bill

Even without expansion, the camp owes daily labor to:
- remain habitable
- remain sanitary enough
- preserve critical reserves
- support continued work tomorrow

## 5.2 Signs the maintenance bill is unpaid
- rising clutter
- damp bedding backlog
- unstable fire continuity
- missing evening water
- food left exposed
- waste drifting too close to living space
- increased minor injury / sickness burden
- more frantic emergency tasks

## 5.3 Small neglect should compound

One skipped routine is usually survivable.
Repeated neglect should snowball into:
- bad sleep
- lower productivity
- higher contamination
- reserve damage
- social frustration
- more emergency interruption

---

# 6. Player control over routine

The player should influence routine mostly through policy, priorities, and layout.

## 6.1 Policy examples
- never end the day without minimum fuel staged
- prioritize dry bedding before low-value hauling
- maintain one clean eating / prep zone
- secure water before dusk
- halt distant work if camp readiness is too low
- perform storm prep before optional expansion work

## 6.2 Layout examples
- water stored too far from sleep area increases burden
- fuel stored poorly increases nightly instability
- dirty work too close to living space increases contamination
- scattered stockpiles increase search time and disorder

## 6.3 Direct intervention examples
- emergency camp clean-up
- pull all hands to storm securement
- dry bedding immediately
- move food now before rain
- clear a blocked main path

# 7. Readiness concept

## 7.1 Readiness is a better early-game measure than “efficiency”

The player should often be asking:
- is the camp ready for tonight?
- is the camp ready for rain?
- is the camp ready for one injury?
- is the camp ready to support one more person?

That is more grounded and more meaningful than raw productivity.

## 7.2 Readiness should be visible

Useful readiness dimensions:
- sleep readiness
- fuel readiness
- water readiness
- weather readiness
- sanitation readiness
- route readiness
- reserve readiness

---

# 8. Routine progression across the early game

## 8.1 Lone survivor
Routine is fragile and personal.
One missed task can threaten total continuity.

## 8.2 Primitive camp
Routine becomes the first true labor management problem.
The player must choose between:
- stabilizing basics
- chasing additional output

## 8.3 Permanent camp
Routine becomes spatial and organizational.
Layout quality begins to matter much more.

## 8.4 Tiny hamlet
Routine becomes social.
Questions appear like:
- who carries invisible maintenance labor?
- are burdens shared fairly?
- are some people free-riding while others keep the place functioning?

---

# 9. Events and failures that should emerge from routine weakness

- fire goes weak at the worst time because no one staged dry fuel
- everyone sleeps poorly because bedding was never aired
- food spoils because it was left unsorted after return
- the camp feels chaotic because nothing is put back properly
- a rain event reveals that visible progress hid a fragile domestic foundation
- one newcomer makes everything harder because the routine margin was already too thin

These are excellent early-game stories because they come from believable neglect.

# 10. UI and readability requirements

The player should be able to see:
- what recurring tasks are falling behind
- what is merely messy versus what is dangerous
- whether the camp is “ready for night”
- which domestic burden is consuming labor
- who is repeatedly carrying camp maintenance work
- what routine failure caused the latest visible problem

Useful readouts:
- dry bedding available tonight
- staged fuel level
- evening safe water level
- sanitation backlog
- storage disorder level
- camp readiness summary

# 12. Minimum viable implementation for the first playable slice

The first implementation does not need every possible chore.
It does need a believable recurring maintenance burden.

Minimum valuable routine categories:
- fire continuity
- water staging
- bedding dryness
- food protection
- waste separation
- evening camp readiness

If those exist and can visibly succeed or fail, the camp will already feel much more alive.

---

# 13. Final design rule

The early game should teach that **survival is not only about acquiring things**.

It is also about:
- protecting them
- arranging them
- checking them
- drying them
- cleaning around them
- staging them for the next day

A settlement becomes believable when maintenance is visible, consequential, and worth the player’s attention.
